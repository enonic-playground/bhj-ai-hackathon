### Objective and exit demonstration

Deliver a playable single-word round using the existing maze: Start → chase a moving ball → catch → correct guess stays in Guessing → wrong guess triggers a countdown and a new chase → catch again → solve the word → show a result and allow a fresh round. Preserve M1 movement, dots, scoring, controls, and resize behavior.

### In scope

1. **Ball movement and appearance.** Add one clearly distinguishable rolling target, moving at 80% of configured player speed. It follows player-walkable corridors and tunnels, never enters walls/home/door tiles, never consumes dots, and cannot stop indefinitely at a dead end. Choose a legal direction at tile centres; use seeded/injected randomness for tests. A reasonable initial policy is to exclude immediate reversal when another exit exists and reverse at dead ends. Document the chosen policy. Simulate movement independently of render cadence.
2. **Safe spawns.** On round start and after each wrong letter, select a random reachable corridor at least six shortest-path tiles from the player, using legal maze edges including tunnel links. Exclude the player's occupied tile, walls and home/door tiles. If the player is between centres, use a documented consistent graph anchor and also exclude adjacent occupied segment endpoints so a spawn cannot overlap the player. If no candidate meets the distance rule, select the farthest remaining reachable eligible cell, randomizing ties. Small test fixtures with no eligible cell must fail clearly or return a handled no-spawn result; never loop forever or spawn on the player. The production maze must always have eligible cells. A respawn may reuse a previous location if it still satisfies the rules.
3. **Capture and freeze.** Detect contact robustly as player and ball move, including opposing travel, delayed render frames, and the tunnel seam. Use swept contact or bounded substeps with a documented capture radius. Do not capture through a wall or compare distant non-tunnel edges as adjacent. Resolve player dots reached before capture, then enter GUESS exactly once and remove the ball. Freeze player position, direction, movement/maze animation, collectible state, and simulation timers throughout GUESS and LEVEL_COMPLETE. Preserve actor position/direction for later resumption; clear queued/held input on transitions.
4. **Word rules.** Select one categorized common English word of 4–8 ASCII letters at round start and retain it across chases. Include a small local seed list (for example 6–10 entries, including repeated-letter words) so the round is playable; the 50-word bank, no-repeat campaign selection, difficulty progression, and five-level campaign are M4. Inject word selection for tests. Show category, mask, and guessed-letter state in the HUD/panel. Accept only individual A–Z letters, case-insensitively. A correct guess reveals every occurrence and awards 100 points per newly revealed position; keep GUESS active unless complete. Duplicate or invalid input has no effect. Record each wrong letter and return to chasing without reducing score or inventing a life penalty.
5. **Resume transition.** Use a configurable two-second initial countdown after a wrong letter, showing the missed letter and “Catch the ball again.” Select the new ball spawn once when entering RESUMING; player and ball stay frozen until the countdown completes. Only the countdown advances in RESUMING. Ignore movement and letter requests during it, and clear input again on exit. Then return to CHASE from the preserved player position/direction. Process transitions centrally so rapid clicks, key repeat, and repeated update calls cannot trigger multiple respawns or duplicate bonuses.
6. **Round completion.** The final correct letter transitions once to LEVEL_COMPLETE, awards the 1,000-point word bonus once, and displays the solved word, word bonus, and total score. For this single-round milestone, offer “Play again” (fresh round, score/dots/actors/word/guesses reset) and “Title screen.” Do not expose an inactive Next level button or simulate the five-level campaign. A new round may choose the same word; campaign uniqueness arrives in M4. Clearing dots alone never completes a round and must not disable ball spawning/capture.
7. **Input and focus.** CHASE owns arrows/WASD and the movement pad; GUESS owns physical letter keys and the accessible HTML letter grid. Thus W/A/S/D guess letters in GUESS without moving the player. Keep shortcuts with Ctrl/Meta/Alt available to the browser. Suppress browser defaults for owned movement keys even when ignoring auto-repeat, while preventing held/repeated input from leaking into a newly entered mode. Ignore movement in GUESS and guesses in CHASE/RESUMING/results. Touch guesses and physical keys use identical rules. A pointer event must not also guess the same letter or activate a replacement control after a mode switch.
8. **Responsive guessing UI.** Use the accepted stacked mobile overlay and desktop sidebar. Keep score, mode, level, category, and word progress readable. Use at least 44 × 44 px letter buttons, disabled guessed letters with non-color-only hit/miss feedback, and visible focus. Keep all 26 letters accessible at 360 × 640 without invoking a native keyboard; preserve M1 tall-to-short resizing. Move focus into the guessing panel on capture and to a sensible enabled letter after a correct guess; announce word/guess feedback through a live region. On resumption, focus the play region; on completion, focus the result heading or primary action. Hidden panels must not retain interactive controls in the tab order. Do not add fake lives, mute, or pause controls.
9. **Background timing.** Preserve M1's safe background-time handling for the new states too: hidden time must not run down RESUMING or replay accumulated maze time on return. Clear input on focus loss and avoid advancing frozen modes via render callbacks. Full PAUSED state, explicit resume on focus return, and the pause menu remain M3, as staged in M1; do not claim those are implemented yet.
10. **R3 cleanup.** Regenerate root lockfile metadata so `packages[""].engines.node` matches `package.json` (`^22.12.0 || ^24.0.0 || >=26.0.0`). Do not upgrade dependencies to accomplish this. ### Deterministic browser fixture and production isolation

`src/app/fixture.ts` reads three query parameters once at start-up: `testSeed=<integer>` seeds the round's randomness, `testWord=<index>` pins the word to an entry of `SEED_WORDS`, and `testBall=off` starts a round with no ball. They are ignored unless present and well formed, so an ordinary visit is unaffected. They choose only what a round starts with: nothing mutates a running game, no answer is exposed in the page or the snapshot, and no rule is bypassed. `testBall=off` is used only by the inherited M1 movement and layout journeys. The M2 journeys use `/?testWord=0&testSeed=7` and rely on the real spawn rule and the real capture algorithm; the test reads the public snapshot, computes a turn with the maze's own traversal rules, and presses a key or taps the pad, so the player is never teleported and capture is never triggered directly. The snapshot masks unrevealed letters and exposes the answer only once the round is over, so a browser test cannot read the word out of the page.

### Findings

**M2-R1 — P2 — Discard the suspended frame interval on visibility return.** `src/app/shell.ts:317–335`: visibility handlers reset the fixed-step accumulator but retain `previousTime`. If animation frames stop while the page is hidden, the first visible frame passes the entire absence to `loop.advance`. Its catch-up clamp still consumes about 100 ms of the countdown; with less than that remaining, the game leaves RESUMING immediately and can move actors in the remaining steps. This violates the M2 background-timing requirement. Reset/rebase the frame timestamp across visibility changes so the first return frame contributes no hidden time. Add a shell-level regression that suspends frame delivery while hidden, returns with less than 100 ms left on the countdown, and checks the countdown and actors remain unchanged until subsequent visible time.

Independent reproduction: an in-memory Node harness ran the actual shell block from `dropHeldInput` through `frame`, with the actual `FixedStepLoop`, mocked visibility/RAF, and a countdown sink. Deliver frame at 1000 ms, hide, deliver no frames, show, deliver frame at 61000 ms: a 50 ms remaining countdown became 0 instead of staying 50. This is a deterministic clock-wiring reproduction, not an actual background-tab device test. The existing test “cannot replay a long absence into the countdown” explicitly expects 100 ms to be spent, so it does not enforce the brief. The first harness attempt failed because TypeScript 7 did not expose the expected transpilation API; rerunning with Node's `stripTypeScriptTypes` succeeded. Repository checks were unaffected.

**M2-R2 — P2 — Isolate browser fixtures from the ordinary production build.** `src/app/shell.ts:76` and `src/app/fixture.ts:41–43`: every production launch reads fixture query parameters. Visiting `/?testBall=off` disables every ball spawn, leaving a playable-looking round with no route to guessing or completion; `testWord` also pins the answer selection. These are active production controls, despite the handoff's isolation claim. Gate fixture configuration behind an explicit test-only build/entry and make ordinary production builds ignore it. Preserve deterministic browser tests and add a check against ordinary production output proving these parameters cannot disable the ball or override word selection. ### M2-R1 — Discard the suspended frame interval on visibility return

- Status: RESOLVED.
- Reproduced first, on `f1e58b35`, with the mechanism as reported: the visibility handlers dropped the loop's accumulator but kept `previousTime`, so the first frame after an absence handed the whole absence to `loop.advance`, which spent its catch-up bound of `simulationStepMs × maxStepsPerFrame` = 100 ms on it. With less than 100 ms left, the countdown ended and actors moved because the tab came back.
- Change: frame timing moves out of the shell into `src/app/frameTiming.ts`. `FrameTiming.suspend()` clears the frame timestamp as well as calling the loop's reset, so the next frame is treated as the first one and contributes nothing; `FrameTiming.frame(time)` advances the loop only when the page is visible and suspends itself when a frame still arrives while hidden. `src/app/shell.ts` now only wires the DOM to it: window blur and the hidden branch of `visibilitychange` drop held input and suspend, the visible branch suspends, and the animation callback calls `timing.frame(time)`. Hidden time therefore never reaches the simulation at all, rather than being clamped.
