### Objective and boundaries

Deliver a complete one-level game with both win and loss paths: chase while avoiding four enemies; collect a power pellet and eat an enemy; catch/guess/miss without losing arcade state; survive a death with progress intact; lose all lives and restart; pause any active mode and explicitly resume it.

Keep the existing single-word seed list and Play again/Title screen result flow. M4 owns the 50-word bank, five-level campaign, fruit, extra life, persistent high score/preferences, sound and campaign pacing. M3 owns pellet and frightened-enemy scoring because those interactions must work here. M5 owns PWA and required real-device release checks. No backend, dependencies upgrade, framework replacement, arcade-perfect emulation or new maze design is required.

### Implementation contract

The following are implementation defaults within the accepted simplified arcade scope (D013), not claims of original arcade fidelity. Keep tuning values in configuration and document any adjustments and their rationale.

1. **Enemy traversal and home.** Add exactly four independently simulated enemies, each with a stable identity and distinguishable visual marker, separate from the ringed ball. Extend traversal with explicit permissions for roaming, exiting and returning enemies; never make home/door player- or ball-walkable. Reuse the existing authored home and door, adding validated spawn/exit/home metadata as needed. Normal enemies roam corridors/tunnels; only exiting/returning actors cross the door. Validate all spawn, release and return routes, including reachability from the roaming maze. Waiting/returning enemies cannot damage the player or be eaten. No enemy consumes collectibles or interacts with the ball. Preserve the player's original maze paths and dot behavior.
2. **Four behaviors and phases.** At tile centres, choose legal turns using path distances over the appropriate graph, deterministic tie-breaking, and no immediate reversal unless required at a dead end or by an explicitly documented mode transition. During CHASE phase: direct pursuit targets the player's nearest legal tile; ambush targets up to four legal steps ahead along the player's direction, stopping at a blocked step (stationary player: current tile); patrol cycles through configured reachable waypoints; proximity pursuit targets the player when at least eight path tiles away and retreats to its scatter target when nearer. Distinctness must be demonstrable in small deterministic fixtures, not just different colors. Scatter targets are four configured reachable corners; start with repeating 7-second scatter / 20-second chase phases. Initial roaming speed: 75% of player speed; frightened: 50%; returning: 125%. Keep ball speed at 80%. Avoid pathfinding every render frame; share graph helpers without weakening traversal permissions.
3. **Release and return lifecycle.** Initial/death-reset release offsets: 0, 2, 4 and 6 active CHASE seconds; all four must visibly leave home. An eaten enemy immediately becomes harmless and returns by a legal path, waits one active second at home, exits and rejoins play. Never teleport through walls, disappear permanently, or get stuck at the door. Release/return waits freeze outside CHASE. A rejoining enemy inherits a currently active frightened effect; otherwise it uses the current chase/scatter phase. Document deterministic tie/phase reversal choices.
4. **Power pellets and frightened behavior.** Place four pellets on existing reachable outer corridors (suggested zero-based cells: `(1,1)`, `(19,1)`, `(1,21)`, `(19,21)`). Replace the dot at each location; do not award both scores. A pellet is consumed once for 50 points and activates/refreshes six active CHASE seconds of frightened time, resetting the enemy-score chain. Roaming frightened enemies use seeded legal random movement and a distinct visual state. Eating enemies awards 200, 400, 800, then 1,600 points; cap subsequent eats within the same effect at 1,600. Return/wait states are harmless regardless of frightened expiry. Freeze the chase/scatter phase clock while frightened; resume its remaining time on expiry. A second pellet refreshes duration rather than stacking it. Expiry and timer-boundary ordering must be documented and tested. Pellets stay consumed across death and do not decide level completion.
5. **Collision ordering.** Extend M2's bounded movement/contact approach to every actor, including the fastest returning actor. Resolve each simulation substep in PRD order: legal movement → reached collectibles → enemy contacts → ball capture if alive. A pellet reached on that substep protects before contact; lethal enemy contact beats simultaneous ball capture. Determine whether any contact is lethal before awarding frightened-enemy eats for that substep. Multiple lethal contacts cost only one life; one enemy contact can award only once before that enemy enters returning state. Protection, walls, opposing motion and tunnel-seam distances must work consistently. Death/capture ends the substep processing for that state so later work cannot leak into a frozen mode.
6. **Lives, death and restart.** Start fresh runs with three lives. Lethal contact decrements once and enters DYING; freeze the maze and ignore gameplay input during a configurable short death presentation (initially 750 ms), advancing only its own timer. With lives remaining, reset player/enemies to starts, clear queued/held input, respawn one ball using M2's safe-spawn rule, reset enemy release/phase/frightened timers and enemy-score chain, then return to CHASE with two active CHASE seconds of visible protection. Preserve score, word, correct/wrong guesses, consumed dots and pellets. Protection ignores lethal contacts, not ordinary collection/ball capture; it freezes during GUESS/RESUMING/PAUSED. At zero lives, show GAME_OVER with answer and final score, Restart run and Title screen; no further maze/timer/score changes. Restart and Play again create a fully fresh three-life run, without stale return timers, protection, guesses or collectibles. A wrong letter still costs no life. Solving the word still completes immediately, independent of remaining enemies/dots/pellets.
7. **Pause and focus loss.** Implement PAUSED with a retained prior state and remaining timers. A visible Pause button and Escape pause CHASE, GUESS, RESUMING or DYING. Blur or hidden visibility must also pause these states, including a visible window that loses focus while RAF continues. Repeated blur/visibility events must not overwrite the retained state with PAUSED. TITLE, LEVEL_COMPLETE and GAME_OVER remain terminal/menu states and need no additional pause overlay. Every timer, actor and maze animation freezes while paused, and ordinary gameplay inputs are ignored. Focus/visibility return never resumes automatically; only a deliberate Resume action does. Ignore auto-repeat and prevent an event that opened pause from also activating Resume. Resume restores the exact prior state (GUESS remains GUESS; RESUMING/DYING continue their remaining duration), rebases the frame clock and clears held/queued inputs. Resuming while still hidden must not advance play. Provide Restart run and Title screen in the pause menu; these discard the current run explicitly through their labeled actions. No fake mute control.
8. **UI and regression baseline.** Add accurate lives and enemy/pellet visuals, death/protection feedback, accessible pause/game-over controls and final-word reveal. Keep HUD word progress and all controls readable at 360 × 640 and desktop, with targets at least 44 × 44 and M1 height-resize behavior intact. Pause overlays own keyboard focus and exclude underlying controls from the tab order; restore sensible focus for each resumed state. GUESS still owns all A–Z (including P/R); use Escape or buttons for pause so no letter is stolen. Keep platform shortcuts intact. Clear movement/pointer state across death, pause, restart and guess transitions; all overlays disable irrelevant input. Update instructions and README with actual M3 behavior, specifically correcting M2's overly broad “unfocused” claim through a real focus-loss pause implementation.

### Summary

Traversal is now an explicit permission on the movement engine rather than a property of an actor. `maze` is the shared player, ball and roaming-enemy graph; `home` is that graph plus the enemy door and interior, and only an enemy in its `exiting` or `returning` state ever asks for it. Because `home` is a superset, a state change part-way through a move can never make an illegal move legal, and widening enemy movement cannot widen the player's or the ball's.

`LEVEL_ONE_LAYOUT` gains four `E` start slots around the tile below the existing door, and four `o` power pellets on the outer corridors at `(1, 1)`, `(19, 1)`, `(1, 21)` and `(19, 21)`, each replacing the dot that was there. The loader derives the door, the single corridor outside it and the single home tile inside it, and rejects a layout whose routes do not exist. `Game` additionally rejects more enemies than slots, an unreachable scatter corner or patrol waypoint, and a patroller with no circuit.

`src/game/enemy.ts` holds the five enemy states, the four targeting policies and the turn choice; `src/game/paths.ts` holds the breadth-first distance helper and a per-target memo shared by all four enemies. `Game` gains the pellet set, the frightened effect, the phase clock, lives, the death presentation, protection and PAUSED, and its step resolves each slice in the PRD's order. The shell gains lives, a Pause control, pause, death and game-over surfaces and the focus rules for them; the renderer gains enemies, pellets, protection and death feedback. Design choices and their reasons are recorded as [D014](../DECISIONS.md#d014--m3-implementation-choices).
